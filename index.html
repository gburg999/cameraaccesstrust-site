How it works · our planned design

Four steps, every time someone looks.

Think of a sign-in sheet at a front desk. Ours is designed to do the same for every search of camera or plate data — and the record is kept by someone independent of the camera company.

1

Ask

Every search starts with a reason.

Who (checked digital ID) and a case number or reason become part of the record.
2

Check

Every request is checked against the rules, and the result is recorded.

Warrant, role, area, state law. Allowed or denied — denials recorded too. We do not claim we always block access before data is shown.
3

Seal

Seal the record.

A digital signature. Change anything and it stops matching.
4

Receipt

Post a public receipt.

A short summary + a fingerprint on a public ledger. Never video, names or plates.

Any change would show. Video stays private.

FAQ

Straight answers.

Does this put video or license plates on the internet?

No. It’s designed so video and plate data stay in the city’s own storage. Public receipts and ledger fingerprints never include names, plates or video.

Who can see the full record?

The city and approved auditors (for a set period). The design gives no one a single master key.

Do you stop an officer from looking?

In our design, every request is checked against the rules, and the result (allowed or denied) is recorded. Denied requests are recorded too. We do not claim on this page that we always block access before data is shown.

Is this live in my city?

Not yet. CameraAccessTrust is in development. If you represent an agency, ask about a pilot.

What does “patent pending” mean?

A U.S. provisional patent application was filed in October 2026. We do not publish the application number on this site.

Are you related to Flock Safety?

No. We are independent of camera vendors. We plan to connect across brands — Flock first, then others.
